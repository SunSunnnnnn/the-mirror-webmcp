# The Mirror — Final Voiceover

Target delivery: calm, intelligent, curious. Estimated runtime: **2:42–2:48** at a natural pace.

## Script

AI can already find a stylist's portfolio, reviews, prices, and services. But none of those tells an agent how the stylist thinks.

A finished image shows the result, not what the stylist protected, what they refused, or why a constraint became part of the design.

The Mirror is a professional-discovery experiment built around judgment evidence. It uses WebMCP—a way for a website to offer structured tools directly to an AI agent.

Our visitor says: “I need to wear my hair up at work and can't look too dramatic, but outside work I don't want to feel boring. I also don't want frequent maintenance. Can you help me decide whether this stylist might understand what I'm looking for?”

The first tool returns strong fit evidence: designed visibility, identity boundaries, and low-maintenance thinking. It surfaces two relevant historical cases.

The agent is not choosing a hairstyle. It is finding evidence that this stylist has previously reasoned about the same kind of human problem.

Next, the agent opens public case zero-zero-three.

The context is practical: the client's hair had to be worn up at work without looking too dramatic. But the underlying need was psychological. She did not want to choose between a professional identity and a more playful private self.

SunSun's judgment was to treat visibility itself as a design variable. A restrained outer layer could support expression inside, while movement, distance, and wearing the hair down changed how that expression was discovered.

A portfolio image could show the colour. The tool exposes why the colour was placed there.

Finally, the agent builds a pre-consultation intent brief. It records the professional and private identity tension, the low-maintenance preference, the desired visibility, the cases that resonated, and the questions still unresolved.

It does not produce a formula or technical plan. It helps the visitor understand why this professional may be worth consulting.

At home, neither the visitor nor the agent can reliably verify strand integrity, chemical history, or achievable lift. So past hair conditions are evidence of how SunSun made decisions—not a template for diagnosing a stranger. The actual technical plan still requires an in-person assessment.

The Mirror does not choose your hair. It reveals how the stylist thinks.

The Mirror makes professional judgment machine-readable.
